Keep cats and dogs recognizable and anatomically believable while gently simplifying their forms. Allow modest exaggeration in ears, posture, silhouettes, and gestures to strengthen personality.

Give each animal distinctive proportions, an individual expression, and small natural asymmetries. Let the drawing feel observed and interpreted by an illustrator. Preserve charm without oversized baby eyes or exaggerated cartoon proportions.

PAINT HANDLING

Build forms with broad, economical brushstrokes. Keep the direction, shape, and occasional broken edge of each stroke visible.

Use layered opaque colour, dry-brush marks, patches of uneven paint coverage, and small glimpses of warm underpainting. Let these marks describe the direction of fur, the planes of a face, the folds of fabric, or the shape of foliage.

Paint fur as grouped tufts and interlocking colour shapes, using only a few individual hairs at important edges. Suggest grass, leaves, stone, and wood through expressive marks and simplified patterns.

Vary colour subtly within the light and shadow areas while preserving clear, readable forms. Allow some painted passages to remain loose and spontaneous.

DETAIL AND EDGES

Concentrate precision around the eyes, muzzle, important paws, and objects essential to the scene. Use broader, simpler handling elsewhere.

Vary the edges: a few crisp accents, some softly broken contours, and occasional edges that merge gently into the surroundings. Allow restrained traces of warm brown drawing to remain visible in selected places.

Keep the overall illustration finished and intentional, with enough detail to tell its story clearly at card size. Preserve visible brush decisions instead of smoothing every passage to the same finish.

COLOUR AND LIGHT

Use a cohesive palette of cream, ochre, muted russet, moss green, dusty blue, warm grey, and natural coat colours, with a few richer accents appropriate to the card.

Describe light through painted shapes, colour relationships, and selective highlights. Keep surfaces mostly matte, with restrained reflections and soft atmospheric depth.

Vary the palette and lighting between scenes while maintaining the same brushwork and approach to colour.

FINISH

Aim for the character of a carefully composed fantasy book illustration: expressive, slightly rough in its paint handling, and rich in personality.

Avoid photographic fur, uniformly sharp detail, glossy 3D shading, perfectly smooth gradients, excessive rim lighting, artificial bloom, and repeated tiny texture marks. Create texture through meaningful brushwork; avoid blanket grain, blur, distressing, or an applied “painting filter.”

Apply this treatment to the illustration. Render card typography, mana symbols, and frame elements with clean print precision.
